Integrations

Every site is already wired for the one button that matters.

A plumber needs a booking slot. A bakery needs an order. A food bank needs a donation. It is nearly always one action, and the site is worth very little if that action is a dead link. So twenty-nine of the thirty ship with the panel for that one action already built — and the thirtieth, a SaaS product template, ships twenty of them.

Donations

The rescue, the food bank, the river trust and the scholarship fund. A giving form has to open in one tap from anywhere on the page, so it does.

Takes an embed from Stripe · JustGiving · Donorbox

The thirtieth is the exception, on purpose. Cadence is a software product template rather than a local business, so instead of one action it carries twenty mount points — assistant, live chat, exit-intent capture, payments, CRM, analytics, ticketing and the rest. Same rule applies to every one of them.

The slot ships empty, and that is deliberate. I have no arrangement with any of those companies and I will not sign you up to one. You choose the provider, on your own account, at your own price — then the embed goes into a single documented place in the file and nothing about the layout changes. What is already done is the part that is fiddly to get right: the panel opens from any button on the page, moves keyboard focus into itself, traps Tab while it is open, closes on Escape, hands focus back to the button you came from, and stops the page behind it scrolling. That was checked on every site, at phone width and desktop width.

How it works

Four stages, and you're talking to me at all of them.

Freelance means no handoffs. The person who scopes the job is the person who writes the code and the person who answers the phone afterwards.

Stage one

A conversation, not a pitch

Free · about an hour

Tell me the trade and what customers actually ring up to ask. I come back with a structure before anything gets designed — and occasionally with the advice that you don't need a new site at all.

Stage two

Fixed quote, in writing

Before any work starts

One number, one scope, one date. If the scope changes later we agree the change first. No hourly billing and no invoice that arrives bigger than the quote.

Stage three

Build, in the open

Typically two to three weeks

You get a link on day one and watch it come together. Feedback lands as comments rather than a formal review round, which is faster for both of us.

Stage four

Handover you actually own

Files, hosting, and no lock-in

Hand-written HTML and CSS with no framework and no build step. Any developer can pick it up, including one who isn't me. That is deliberate — a site you can only maintain through me isn't yours.
