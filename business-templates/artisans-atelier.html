The studio

Why the gallery is at the front and the mess is at the back.

Most galleries hide the making. The work arrives finished, hung under careful light, with no evidence that anyone got their hands dirty producing it. We have never understood the appeal of that.

Here the front room is the gallery and the back room is the studio, separated by a wide doorway that stays open. If you come on a Thursday you will probably interrupt something — a panel being gessoed, a plate being inked, a stretcher being squared up on the bench.

That is deliberate. Seeing the ground being laid on a panel changes how the finished surface reads. Watching a monotype pulled — and seeing that the second pull is fainter and the third barely there — explains an edition of eight far better than a wall text ever could.

It also keeps us honest. It is considerably harder to overstate what went into a piece when the person asking can see the bench it was made on.

Programme

Workshops at the bench.

Six people at a time, taught where the work is made. All materials provided, and you take home whatever you produce — including the failures, which are usually more instructive.

WS 01

Monotype, one evening

Ink, plate, press. You will pull perhaps six prints and three of them will be worth keeping, which is a normal ratio.

Thursdays, 6.30–9.30pm $95
WS 02

Preparing panels

Rabbit-skin glue, gesso, sanding between coats. Unglamorous, and it changes everything about how paint sits.

First Saturday, 10am–1pm $110
WS 03

Egg tempera, four weeks

Grinding pigment, mixing with yolk, building in layers. A slow medium taught at its own pace.

Tuesdays × 4, 6.30–9pm $340
WS 04

Drawing from the still life

Charcoal and chalk on toned paper, working from a set-up that stays up all month. Beginners entirely welcome.

Sundays, 2–5pm $80
Commissions

How a commission works.

Roughly two commissions run at a time, so the waiting list is real. If a date matters, say so early rather than late.

Conversation

In the studio if you can manage it, so you can see surfaces and scale in person rather than guessing from a screen.

Written proposal

Medium, dimensions, timeline and price, in writing. A deposit of one third confirms the slot.

Studies

Two or three small studies before the final piece begins. You choose the direction, and you can say none of them.

Making

Photographs at each significant stage. No surprises at the end — you will have seen it become what it is.

Delivery

Framed if you want it framed, delivered and hung locally, with care notes for the surface.
