From the roastery — 21 Kiln Lane

Read the menuOrder coffee Find us

We opened in 2021 with one drum roaster, a second-hand grinder and a fairly stubborn conviction that the gap between roasting and pouring should be measured in metres rather than in weeks of freight.

That conviction turned out to be the whole business. Coffee is at its best in a window that opens a few days after roasting and closes well before most bags reach a shelf. Shortening that window is not a marketing angle. It is simply the difference between a cup that tastes of something and one that tastes of brown.

So everything on the bar was roasted in this building, usually within the past week. Retail bags carry a roast date rather than a best-before, because a best-before tells you when coffee becomes unpleasant, not when it was good.

We buy green coffee through importers who publish what they paid the producer, and we publish what we paid them. There are four single origins on the bar at any time and they rotate as lots run out — which is the honest reason the filter changes rather than a curatorial flourish.

The café side runs from six in the morning because a great many people need coffee before seven and very few places are willing to be open for them. The kitchen runs until two. The roaster runs Mondays and Thursdays, and anyone standing at the counter is welcome to ask what is going on.

None of this is complicated. It is mostly a matter of doing the ordinary things — buying carefully, roasting recently, and not letting anything sit — with more attention than is strictly convenient.

Method

From green bean to your cup

  1. Green coffee arrives

    Bought through importers who publish what they paid the producer. We publish what we paid the importer.

  2. Sample roasted and cupped

    Every lot is roasted small and tasted blind against the last one before we commit a single sack to the bar.

  3. Production roast, Mondays and Thursdays

    Twelve-kilo batches on the drum at the back of the room. Stand at the counter and ask — whoever's on it will explain.

  4. Rested, then poured

    Espresso rests four days, filter about seven. Nothing goes on the bar before it's ready or long after it stops being good.

  5. Bagged with a roast date

    Retail bags carry the date it was roasted, not the date it becomes unpleasant. Orders ship every Wednesday.
